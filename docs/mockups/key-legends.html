Key legends: one scheme

Nothing on a key moves. The face is the tap; the top-left text is Shift (and the flick up); the bottom-left letter is Alpha (and the flick down). What the next press does is lit, the rest dims.

  1. Armed is a ring, locked is a fill. Shift armed: sin⁻¹; locked: sin⁻¹. Alpha: G and G. The bar's ⇧/α sign and the Shift and Alpha keys use the same ring and fill.
  2. The faces dim while a layer is armed, except where that layer is the tap: Alpha on a key with no letter does its tap, so Esc, Undo, Fmt, . and ⌫ stay lit.
  3. An empty corner is an empty layer. Shift armed and nothing lit on a key: that press does nothing (7, a b/c and Fmt are reserved).
  4. Flicks get no mark: the corner is the flick. Two exceptions, said in Help: ⌫ and the round pad have none.
  5. Orange is Shift's only (docs/05 3.10); letters are Alpha's colour, in italics (R142). A user key's Alpha slot sits top right, as rows 0–2 have no letter line. In CAS the letter line is lower case.
