2. **The change:**
   - **`oracle.py`, `check: "points"`:**
     - The row's `expect` (Giac's text, read with `check_exact`'s reader) and `oracle` (an mpmath lambda of the free names) are evaluated at 7 seeded points per name, in `points: [lo, hi]` (default [0.5, 3]; `int` for `n`).
     - They must agree to 40 digits at 60.
     - With `upto: "constant"`, the expect's mpmath `diff` is compared to the oracle (antiderivatives), and `desolve`'s `c_0` is drawn as one more name.
   - **`oracle.py`, `check: "set"`:**
     - The `expect` is read as a predicate: a list is an or of its elements; `and`, `<`, `<=`, `>`, `>=` as in Giac.
     - It is compared with the oracle's predicate at 50 seeded points across `points`, and at each boundary ± 10^-20.
     - The boundary points themselves, strict or not, are `derived` with a `note`: at a root of x²−2, mpmath decides nothing.
   - **The oracle for each row is built from the input, never from the answer:**
     - `diff` by mpmath's `diff`;
     - `int` by the derivative against the integrand;
     - `simplify`, `expand` and the display forms by the input itself;
     - `desolve` by its equation's residual.
   - **Derived, with a note:** `factor(x^3-2*x-5)` (irreducible: no rational root among ±1, ±5). A `rootof` row becomes rule C's count row `size(…)`, as `precision.yaml:1575`. The input `rootof(…)` returned unchanged (`e5`, `e27`, `e28`) is derived.
   - **Rows** go into `precision.yaml`, `kind: "cas"`, under G509's header, through the converter with `--oracles`.
