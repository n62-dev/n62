- **What happens:** Home and CAS `int(tan(1/x)/x^2,x,1,2)` show `GIAC_ERROR: Unable to handle singularities of -1/2*ln(tan(1/x)^2+1) at [-2/(-2*n_1*pi-pi)] Error: Bad Argument Value`; CAS `approx(int(…))` shows the same. - **Same:** `int(tan(sqrt(x))/sqrt(x),x,1,2)` (right 2 ln cos 1 − 2 ln cos √2 = 2.48526759498), `int(1/(x^2*cos(1/x)^2),x,1,2)` and `int(sec(1/x)^2/x^2,x,1,2)` (right tan 1 − tan(1/2) = 1.01110523481; Giac's words end in `at [undef]`).
- **Right today:** the indefinite `int(tan(1/x)/x^2,x)` = `-1/2*ln(tan(1/x)^2+1)`; integrals whose singular family Giac can solve (`int(x*tan(x^2),x,0,1)`, `int(tan(2*x+1),x,0,1/5)`, `int(cot(x),x,1,2)`); a real pole inside is still refused (`int(exp(x)*tan(exp(x)),x,0,1/2)`: e^x = π/2 at x = ln(π/2) = 0.4516).
- **Cause (Giac, kept):** for the definite integral Giac checks the antiderivative's singular points in [a, b]; when `solve` returns them as a family in an integer parameter (`n_1`) or `undef`, it gives up with this error (intg.cc, the definite branch of `_integrate`; to pin down in step 1). Deciding which members of a parametric family fall in [a, b] is outside what Giac does today.
