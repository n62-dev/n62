- **What happens:** Home or CAS, `assume(x>0)`, then `int(cos(x)/x,x,-1,1)` shows `0` with `=` (Home: exact path, exact 0). The integral does not converge (`source: derived`: cos x/x ~ 1/x at 0; without the assumption N62 says "The integral does not converge").
- **The same `= 0`** after `assume(x>0)`: `int(1/x,x,-1,1)`, `int(1/x^3,x,-1,1)`, `int(1/tan(x),x,-1,1)`, `int(1/sin(x)^3,x,-1,1)`, `int(sin(x)/x^4,x,-1,1)`. Every one is refused with no assumption.
- **Right under the assumption:** a pole off the symmetric shortcut is still refused (`int(1/x,x,-1,2)`, `int(1/x^3,x,-1,2)`, `int(tan(x),x,0,3)`); `int(1/x^2,x,-1,1)` is +∞ as with no assumption.
- **Cause (ours):** Giac answers a symmetric interval of an odd integrand with 0, assumption or not (raw `int(1/x,x,-1,1)` = 0): the principal value. G39's check refuses it by looking for the real singular points of f in [a, b]: `app/src/engine/converge.ts:173` `giac.eval(\`singular(${f},${x})\`)`. Giac's `singular` honours the user's assumption on x: raw `assume(x>0)`, `singular(cos(x)/x,x)` = `[]`, while `singular(cos(pask_)/pask_,pask_)` = `[0]`. No point, so the check returns "ok" (converge.ts:175) and the 0 stands.
- **Reach:** `assume(x>0)` is the usual first line before an integral or a limit with a parameter; the name then stays assumed for later lines (G92 keeps a typed assumption).
